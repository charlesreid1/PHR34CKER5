# BBS — BULLETIN BOARD SYSTEMS

Before the web, there were **boards**. A BBS was a single (or few-line)
modem-answering PC running software like WWIV, Renegade, PCBoard, or
Citadel. You dialed in at 300 baud, then 1200, then 2400, eventually 14.4k,
and the machine answered with an ANSI-art welcome screen.

## The culture stack
- **Sysop** ran the board out of a spare bedroom.
- **Users** had handles, not names. Ratios governed downloads.
- **Message bases** were threaded discussion — precursor to Usenet groups.
- **File areas** held shareware, text files, ANSI art, and — on the
  underground boards — t-files, cracks, and phreaking zines.
- **Doors** were external games (Legend of the Red Dragon, Trade Wars 2002).
- **FidoNet** was a store-and-forward internet-of-BBSes over nightly
  scheduled dial-up transfers.

## Why phreaking?
Long-distance was expensive. To dial a board three area codes away every
night, you either paid AT&T or you didn't. The phreaking scene and the BBS
scene share a genealogy for exactly this reason.

## Time capsule
- Peak: roughly 1988–1996.
- Killed by: dial-up ISPs and the web, not by any single event.
